Fabrication of Moiré on Curved Surfaces

Designing and fabricating dynamic, lens-based moiré effects on curved surfaces.

Artin Saberpour, Roger D. Hersch, Jiajing Fang, Rhaleb Zayer, Hans-Peter Seidel, Vahid Babaei

Read paperPublisherWatch video

Research problem

Lens-based level-line moiré effects are typically designed on planar substrates. Curvature changes lens placement, focal geometry, and the relationship between the revealing and base layers, introducing unwanted artifacts when naïvely extending planar arrangements.

Method

We formulated the design of cylindrical-lens arrays on curved surfaces as a low-dimensional geometric design problem. The method aligns lens placement and the underlying base layer while accounting for focal geometry. It also supports configurations in which both optical layers consist of cylindrical lenses.

Results

We evaluated the designs with rendered simulations and physical fabricated examples, including multi-material 3D printing and molding. The accompanying video illustrates the view-dependent motion of the moiré patterns that cannot be conveyed by a still image.
