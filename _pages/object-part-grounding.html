The problem

For a robot handing over an object, recognizing the object is only a first step. A handle, blade, opening, or tool head can carry a different meaning for the interaction. I explored how language-guided vision models can locate such parts and make their visual evidence useful for spatial reasoning.

Approach

I prototyped a text-guided segmentation pipeline that combines Grounding DINO for candidate detection with Segment Anything (SAM) for mask generation. The system accepts descriptions of object parts and produces candidate regions that can be inspected across views.

For simulated handovers, I investigated how masks from orthographic Unity views, together with known object bounds, could be converted into candidate 3D part locations and directions. I also explored vision-language checks for selecting among candidate masks.

Comparing perception approaches

Alongside the grounding pipeline, I explored CLIP for matching part descriptions to image crops and for sliding-window localization. One experiment used negative text prompts and embedding-space projections to investigate finer distinctions between object regions. I also tested OWLv2 for text-conditioned object-part detection. These were separate experiments, rather than a single combined model.

Related perception experiments

I tested YOLOv8-seg on a camera image to detect scene objects and save bounding-box crops. A separate Orient Anything prototype explored image-based orientation estimates and a Python-to-Unity communication interface.

What this work explores

  • Integrating pretrained vision and language models in a perception pipeline.
  • Prompting, visualizing, and inspecting object-part detections and masks.
  • Connecting visual evidence to coordinate systems and geometric representations in Unity.
  • Investigating failure cases when masks or views do not identify the intended part.

These are exploratory prototypes developed during a broader investigation of VLM-guided handovers. The model-assisted approaches shown here are separate from the final method reported in the related paper. The saved examples are qualitative and do not establish a general accuracy measure.
